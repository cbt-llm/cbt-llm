Where do LLMs fall short in CBT-guided reasoning?

LLMs can know CBT theory yet still default to Validation & Reflection when a conversation calls for a different strategy. CBT Lens combines clinical grounding with multiple reasoning paths to choose a response, then measures whether that guidance changes the model’s usual behavior.

To be published in the proceedings of the 14th International Conference on Affective Computing and Intelligent Interaction (ACII 2026) ↗

Explains and suggests

That sounds really heavy to carry—feeling like you have to hide what you’re going through just to avoid being a burden. When someone cares about others, they often start minimizing their own needs. That doesn’t make your feelings less valid—it means you’ve learned to protect others at your own expense.

A few things worth gently questioning:

  • “I’m a burden” → is that a fact, or a fear?
  • If someone you cared about felt this way, would you see them as a burden?
  • Are you giving others the chance to choose?

You don’t have to suddenly open up to everyone. Maybe there’s a small step that feels safe:

  • sharing one honest sentence with someone you trust
  • or even just saying “I’ve been a bit off lately”

You deserve space too—not just to function, but to be real. If you want, we can break this down together:

  • where that “burden” belief started
  • or how to test it in a low-risk way without feeling exposed

03 / The approach

Clinical grounding.

01 — CONCEPTUALIZE

Read the cognitive context

Break a user narrative into elements of Beck’s cognitive conceptualization framework.

02 — GROUND

Retrieve relevant concepts

Connect the user’s context to a SNOMED CT knowledge graph and validate the concepts’ relevance with natural language inference.

03 — SELECT

Choose a CBT strategy

Use multiple chains of thought to select from Validation & Reflection, Socratic Questioning, or an Alternative Perspective.

04 / Protocol Leverage Force

Did our framework change the model’s baseline behavior?

Protocol Leverage Force (F) measures how strongly CBT guidance reorients a response away from the model’s baseline and toward the user’s utterance.

Across three open-weight models and RealCBT-derived user case studies, models often favored Validation & Reflection. F helps quantify how much CBT guidance changed a model’s baseline response. Human reviewers brought expertise in clinical psychology, psychology research, mental health AI, and industry practice.

05 / The affective story

What do valence and arousal show us?

Valence describes how positive or negative an emotion feels. Arousal describes its level of physiological activation or energy. These are the two dimensions of Russell’s Circumplex Model of Affect ↗. The paper compares estimates from user language in CBT-MCoT synthetic sessions with RealCBT human transcripts, scoring each turn with the NRC VAD Lexicon.
